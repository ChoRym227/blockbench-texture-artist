# Combat stance and choreography

1. Inspect actual requested motion references where available; record clip name, actual frames observed, source, and access limits. A catalog title is not motion data. Do not claim mocap retargeting unless performed. If the user supplies FBX, map source bones, local axes, proportions, root motion and weapon grip, then bake and test.
2. Choose weapon-specific idle, locomotion and attack poses. For one-handed heavy sword, default to a lowered guard with elbow near torso, slight stagger, flexed knees and a clear silhouette; a staff upright pose is not a sword guard. For two-handed weapons maintain both grip contacts.
3. Establish a neutral wrist frame from the actual mesh and wrist-to-grip direction. Assess deviations relative to this frame, not just Euler magnitude from bind. Pose the weapon first: target grip point, blade axis, and edge direction; solve shoulder/elbow/wrist with joint limits and preferred elbow plane. Preserve parent hierarchy and local axes; do not use arbitrary rotations or translate the hand away from the arm.
4. Choreograph readable anticipation, acceleration into strike, follow-through and recovery. Inspect world-space blade tip path and edge alignment at intermediate times, not only keyed poses. Turn pelvis and torso with the strike, keep planted feet stationary in world space during stance, and offset cape response. Use intentional timing rather than a uniform speed. Record hit timing for game integration.
5. Keep idle/attack start/end poses compatible and loops continuous; inspect state transitions if possible. Separate in-place animation from entity movement. Check foot and weapon floor clearance, arm-body and cape contacts, wrist bends, and elbow silhouette. Use existing 20 Hz weapon checker plus denser sampling for fast swings and foot-contact review; numerical checks cannot replace visual judgment.
6. Render front, side and three-quarter at least at guard, windup, strike, follow-through and recovery. Provide animated preview from actual model data and compare old/new at matching times when modifying. Disclose untested full-body collision, runtime interpolation, transitions, and ModelEngine/Blockbench compatibility. Keep requested geometry, textures and UV unchanged unless necessary and explicitly report modifications.
